\section*{Background in one paragraph}
We have three supervised RESfM variants, all evaluated on identical tracks, pipeline and
environment. \textbf{RESfM-scratch} is trained by us under our uniform protocol (checkpoint
selected by validation reprojection error, infinity-point margin $10^{-4}$, applied identically to
every arm so the outlier mechanism is the only variable). \textbf{RESfM-faithful} is the same
training with the authors' two released secondary values restored (checkpoint selected by
classification \emph{Accuracy}, margin $10^{-3}$). \textbf{RESfM-released} are the authors'
published weights. Measured differences (translation mean, post-BA):

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
 & MegaDepth & 1DSfM & 1DSfM-hard & Strecha & BlendedMVS & Olsson\\
\midrule
RESfM-scratch (5 seeds) & 0.37$\pm$0.12 & 10.50$\pm$1.45 & 19.16$\pm$3.42 & 0.39$\pm$0.72 & 0.35$\pm$0.04 & 7.37$\pm$2.63\\
RESfM-faithful          & 0.50 & 9.75 & 17.58 & 0.14 & 0.37 & 8.76\\
RESfM-released          & 0.21 & 11.12 & 15.29 & 0.20 & 0.33 & 9.10\\
\midrule
SS-RESfM best arm       & 0.36--0.50 & \textbf{8.93} & \textbf{12.42} & 1.97 & \textbf{0.14} & \textbf{2.91}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The authors' Accuracy rule selects epoch ${\sim}5.5$k; reprojection-based selection
selects ${\sim}16.5$k. Faithful is therefore weaker on five of six datasets: our uniform protocol
\emph{helps} the baseline, and every margin we report against RESfM-scratch is a lower bound.

\section*{Questions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=6pt]
\item \textbf{Which baseline anchors the headline comparison?} RESfM-scratch (stronger, cleanest
control, conservative for us) or RESfM-faithful (the authors' shipped configuration, weaker, gives
us wins on five of six datasets including in-distribution parity)? Our concern with faithful-as-primary:
a reviewer, or the RESfM authors themselves, can observe that we selected checkpoints for our arms
by the better rule while the baseline used the worse one.

\item \textbf{How do we handle the released checkpoint?} It is the strongest supervised artifact
in distribution ($0.209$ vs.\ our $0.36$--$0.50$), yet it \emph{loses} to us out of distribution on
our tracks (1DSfM $11.12$ vs.\ $8.93$; BlendedMVS $0.33$ vs.\ $0.14$) and collapses on Olsson
($9.10$). Reference row plus an explicit ``no SOTA in distribution'' statement, or a stronger framing?

\item \textbf{What is the strongest claim we are willing to defend?} Candidates:
(a) mechanism-isolated study, no SOTA claim (current draft);
(b) ``against RESfM trained from scratch under the authors' released configuration, on identical
tracks, SS-RESfM wins five of six benchmarks'';
(c) something stronger.

\item \textbf{Is the checkpoint-selection result a contribution in its own right?} Classification
accuracy is a poor proxy for reconstruction quality (epoch $5.5$k vs.\ $16.5$k; ${\sim}0.15$ worse
on MegaDepth). It also cannot be used by a label-free method at all, since it requires
ground-truth outlier labels --- which is part of our argument for reprojection-based selection.

\item \textbf{Does the answer differ by venue?} CVPR reviewers tend to expect explicit
state-of-the-art positioning; an ICLR/analysis framing tolerates the scoped version. We currently
plan the mechanism paper for CVPR and a separate label-ceiling/selection paper for ICLR.

\item \textbf{Do we withdraw the 3DV submission?} Withdrawal frees the mechanism paper for CVPR
(deadline after the 3DV decision, so nothing is lost by waiting) but forfeits a likely publication.
Related: are both papers worth running in parallel, or should we consolidate?
\end{enumerate}
