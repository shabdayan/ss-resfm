\section*{The two rules}
Both rows are the \emph{same} supervised RESfM training (27 MegaDepth scenes, same
schedule, same tracks, same robust BA). They differ only in which epoch is kept:
\textbf{(A) ours} minimises \texttt{our\_repro}, the label-free reprojection error of the
network's predicted points under its predicted cameras; \textbf{(B) authors'} maximises
\texttt{Accuracy}, the outlier head's classification agreement with the COLMAP-derived
GT labels. MegaDepth is in-distribution; the other five are out-of-distribution.

\section*{What this shows}
\textbf{1. The rules are statistically indistinguishable everywhere.} The authors'
criterion wins four datasets and ours wins two, every gap is a fraction of the
corresponding seed-to-seed standard deviation, and the sign of the difference flips
across datasets. The single-run pair quoted in the main paper ($0.496$ vs.\ $0.37$ on
MegaDepth) is seed~20, the most extreme of the five and the one most favourable to our
rule; on the other four seeds the authors' rule is better three times.

\textbf{2. Both rules reproduce the Olsson collapse identically} --- four of five seeds
at $8.2$--$9.0$ with exactly one escape --- but the escaping seed differs (s24 for ours,
s22 for theirs). The collapse is a property of the supervised recipe; which seed dodges
it is luck.

\textbf{3. Both reproduce the one-bad-Strecha-seed pattern} (s23: $1.674$ vs.\ $1.556$,
with the other four seeds at $0.005$--$0.222$), and both show the BlendedMVS rotation
pathology ($31.7^\circ$ vs.\ $31.0^\circ$).

\textbf{Conclusion.} Deviating from the authors' checkpoint rule does not advantage us:
if anything their rule gives their own baseline a marginally better band, so every
margin we report against the supervised baseline is a lower bound. Equally important,
the failure modes survive a complete change of selection criterion, so they belong to
the supervised recipe rather than to how we picked the epoch.

\subsection*{What the grids show}
\textbf{1. The supervised Olsson collapse is training-domain-invariant.} Supervision
degrades on Olsson from \emph{every} training pool ($4.96$--$8.62$), while
\textsc{madweight} lands in a tight $2.69$--$3.34$ band from every pool. The collapse is
a property of the recipe, not an artefact of training on MegaDepth.

\textbf{2. The Strecha exception is equally invariant, in the opposite direction.}
Supervision wins Strecha from every pool ($0.39$--$2.37$ vs.\ $2.56$--$3.05$) --- the one
dataset where labels help wherever they come from. It is also the smallest benchmark
($54$ cameras).

\textbf{3. At the $60\%$ target, label-free wins from anywhere.} The best supervised cell
is $18.59$; \textsc{madweight} ranges $15.56$--$19.70$ across pools, and reaches $12.1$
when trained in-domain. No training pool rescues supervision at the extreme.

\textbf{4. MegaDepth as a test set is nearly pool-independent} for both recipes
($0.37$--$0.83$ supervised, $0.47$--$0.63$ label-free), suggesting its curated test
scenes are easy for any reasonably trained model of this family.

\textbf{5. One counterpoint worth stating.} Supervision trained on 1DSfM-hard transfers
to 1DSfM at $8.49$ --- its best cross-domain cell anywhere, and better than
\textsc{madweight} from the same pool ($13.21$). Where labels are sound and the domains
are related, supervision still transfers well.
