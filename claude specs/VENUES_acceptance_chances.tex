\section*{1. The distinction that frames everything}

\textbf{3DV is judged on the frozen August 28 version.} Every other venue would receive the
\emph{strengthened} paper: the causal in-distribution contamination curve, the Olsson
clean-benchmark collapse figures, the cross-generalization invariance grid, the seed-interaction
analysis, and the complete 5-seed baseline bands (ESFM\textsuperscript{*}, vanilla-ESFM, COLMAP,
GLOMAP, GASFM, hybrid). The frozen version predates all of it.

All percentages are one rater's calibration with honest $\pm$10pp error bars. The single biggest
variable is the Ronen co-authorship question (Section~4).

\section*{3. Path probabilities (chance that \emph{some} venue accepts)}

Single-venue rates are not the decision --- the fallback \emph{paths} are. Assuming independent
draws at the per-venue rates above:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Keep-3DV path}: 3DV (45\%) $\rightarrow$ if rejected Dec 2, ICML ($\sim$33\%)
$\rightarrow$ if rejected, ICCV ($\sim$47\%). Chance at least one of the three says yes:
$1-(0.55\times0.67\times0.53)\approx\mathbf{80\%}$.
\item \textbf{Withdraw-for-CVPR path}: CVPR (45\%) $\rightarrow$ if rejected, ICCV ($\sim$47\%).
Chance at least one says yes: $1-(0.55\times0.53)\approx\mathbf{70\%}$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{How to read the 80\% vs.\ 70\%.} The keep-3DV path scores higher \emph{only} because it
contains three draws instead of two --- the pending 3DV submission is a free draw already in
hand. It is \emph{not} a statement that 3DV is easier than CVPR; per-venue both sit at
$\sim$45\%.

\textbf{The value asymmetry that cuts the other way.} The 80\% path's most likely acceptance is
the \emph{frozen August version at 3DV} --- the weakest publishable outcome, which also strands
the strengthened material in a follow-up paper exposed to ``delta over your own 3DV paper''
objections. The 70\% path's acceptances are the \emph{full strengthened paper at CVPR or ICCV}
--- strictly better outcomes. Slightly fewer bites at the apple, but every bite is at a better
apple.

\section*{4. The Ronen branch (the actual decision tree)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Ronen joins as co-author} $\Rightarrow$ withdraw 3DV, submit CVPR
($\sim$50\%+ with him; his involvement converts the political exposure into endorsement).
\item \textbf{Ronen explicitly blesses solo submission} (``submit on your own, acknowledgment is
enough'') $\Rightarrow$ \emph{still} withdraw and submit CVPR. The political risk came from
submitting without his knowledge, not from his absence on the byline; an explicit written
go-ahead removes most of it. Caveats: (a) the blessing fixes the risk, not the rate --- solo
CVPR stays $\sim$45\%; (b) get the blessing to cover the specific act (withdraw 3DV + solo CVPR
+ acknowledgment), in writing; (c) settle Shai's authorship in the same conversation; (d)
``I didn't contribute enough'' is often modesty --- make sure the no is considered (the 3-page
summary exists for exactly this).
\item \textbf{Ronen unasked / lukewarm / ambiguous} $\Rightarrow$ keep 3DV (low-temperature
path): let it decide Dec 2, then ICML (private reviews) $\rightarrow$ ICCV. This is the
$\sim$80\% path, chosen here for risk control, not for expected paper quality.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bottom line}: with either form of explicit Ronen sign-off, take the 70\% path (CVPR) ---
the value asymmetry wins once the politics are defused. Without an answer from him, stay on the
80\% path. Every cell of this table gets better or safer once the conversation has happened;
none of it can be un-submitted afterward.
