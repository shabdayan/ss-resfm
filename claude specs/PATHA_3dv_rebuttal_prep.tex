\section{The learning-rate challenge (highest risk)}
\textbf{Challenge.} ``You train the baseline at RESfM's released $10^{-3}$ but your arms
at $10^{-4}$. Retraining the supervised baseline at $10^{-4}$ may close your
out-of-distribution margins.''

\textbf{What a reviewer would find.} It is true and we have measured it: the baseline
retrained at $10^{-4}$ improves on contaminated OOD --- 1DSfM $5.90$ (vs.\ $11.03$
declared), 1DSfM-hard $12.81$ (vs.\ $16.22$), BlendedMVS $0.14$ (vs.\ $0.38$) --- while
degrading in-distribution ($0.53$ vs.\ $0.34$) and on Strecha ($1.73$ vs.\ $0.006$).

\textbf{Prepared response (four points, all measured).}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{The policy was disclosed, not hidden}: the paper's Protocol section states the
per-method base learning rate and the convergence-timing rationale; neither method is
truncation-limited.
\item \emph{Not an under-training artifact}: a $30$k-epoch replicate at $10^{-4}$
plateaus mid-schedule (best epoch $16$k; never improves through $29{,}999$), so the OOD
gain is a regularization effect of the lower rate, not unfinished training. Its full row:
MegaDepth $0.36$, 1DSfM $6.12$, hard $13.88$, Strecha $1.64$, BMVS $0.18$, Olsson $8.27$.
\item \emph{The asymmetry runs against us, not for us}: the symmetric change on our side
(label-free arms at $10^{-3}$) helps nowhere --- the per-method policy is each method at
its own best-converging rate, not a knob tuned in our favor.
\item \emph{The headline clean-benchmark findings are lr-robust}: the Olsson collapse
persists at both rates ($8.3$--$8.5$) and Strecha degrades at $10^{-4}$; and even against
the strongest supervised variant (the $30$k/$10^{-4}$ row), label-free plain removal
still leads at $60\%$ contamination ($12.4\pm2.1$ vs.\ $13.9$).
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Concession to offer if pressed}: scope the $43\%$ removal-family margin as
``vs.\ the released-configuration baseline''; the removal medians ($1.68\pm0.19$) remain
ahead of the $10^{-4}$ baseline's 1DSfM medians as well.

\section{The single-seed challenge}
\textbf{Challenge.} ``All numbers are seed 20; the conclusions may be seed luck.''

\textbf{Prepared response.} Post-deadline we retrained \emph{every} arm and the baseline
under five seeds ($20$--$24$) and re-ran the full evaluation ($36$ cells, plus a
$2{,}352$-evaluation paired replication where the evaluation seed equals the training
seed). Results (Version B, Sec.~E):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $60\%$ contamination: worst removal seed $15.8$ $<$ best baseline seed $16.2$
(every pairing).
\item $43\%$: every removal median ($1.4$--$1.9$) beats every baseline median
($2.4$--$4.3$).
\item Olsson collapse: $4$/$5$ scratch seeds, plus the released checkpoint ($9.10$) and
three authors-faithful retrains ($8.66$--$9.02$); every mad/weight/remove arm seed lands
at $2.9$--$3.6$.
\item BlendedMVS: mad/weight arms $0.14\pm0.02$ below every baseline seed
($0.35\pm0.04$).
\item Eval-seed control: released weights under five evaluation seeds move by
$\pm0.008$; the paired replication reproduces every band within a fraction of its std.
The variance is in training, amplified by BA --- not in our evaluation.
\item Prior work in this line reports single-seed results (RESfM: ``We fix the seed to
20''); we are, to our knowledge, the first in this benchmark family to report full
training-seed bands.
\end{itemize}

\section{The baseline-fidelity challenge}
\textbf{Challenge.} ``Your RESfM-from-scratch ($0.344$) is weaker than the released
checkpoint ($0.209$) and the paper's $0.169$ --- maybe your baseline is just badly
trained.''

\textbf{Prepared response.} (a) The frozen supplementary already traces the
released-vs-paper gap to environment-level BA basin selection (bidirectional,
scene-concentrated flips; $17$/$36$ scenes \emph{better} in our runs); differences
$\lesssim0.05$ are environment noise. (b) Post-deadline, three authors-faithful retrains
(authors' exact validation metric and margin) give MegaDepth $0.22$--$0.50$ ---
straddling our declared $0.344$ --- while showing the \emph{same} OOD failures (Olsson
$8.7$--$9.0$, BMVS $0.35$--$0.38$). The baseline's in-distribution level is
seed-volatile for every recipe; its clean-OOD failures are recipe- and seed-robust.
(c) The five-seed scratch band ($0.37\pm0.12$) contains the best faithful seeds.

\section{The TTT-nondeterminism challenge}
\textbf{Challenge.} ``Your weight+TTT numbers vary run to run; how much of the paper
rests on that?''

\textbf{Prepared response.} Quantified in the frozen supplementary ($\pm0.07$ on TTT
means; deterministic no-TTT cells reproduce exactly). None of the headline claims
($60\%$, $43\%$, Olsson, BlendedMVS) rests on a TTT cell; the paired five-seed program
confirms the TTT cells are the only ones that move ($\sim0.13$) under evaluation
re-seeding.
